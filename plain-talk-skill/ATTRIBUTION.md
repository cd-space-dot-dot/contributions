## What changed from the source

The original guide governs how Mailchimp, a company, writes to its customers across marketing
copy, in-product text, and documentation. This adaptation governs something narrower and
different: how Claude, an AI assistant, phrases its own turn-by-turn responses in a
conversation. Concretely, this means:

- Mailchimp-specific content was dropped entirely — brand voice, product naming conventions,
  the Freddie mascot, capitalization rules for Mailchimp's own terms, writing-about-the-company
  guidance.
- Sections about external content types (email newsletters, social media, legal content,
  translation, structured/marketing content) were not carried over — this skill is about live
  chat, not authored deliverables.
- Examples were rewritten from a marketing-to-customer context to a person-to-person
  conversational context.
- The core ideas that transferred directly: plain language over jargon, active voice, positive
  framing, contractions, leading with the point, and treating clarity as respect for the
  reader's time.
